## Additional Validation Results — GSE53795

### 1. Internal Cross-Validation

The model was evaluated using 4-fold GroupKFold cross-validation, with patient IDs used as grouping variables to prevent patient overlap between training and validation sets.

Dataset:
- 12 patients
- 24 paired skin samples
- 12 lesional samples
- 12 non-lesional samples
- 54,675 microarray probe sets per sample

Results:
- Mean cross-validation accuracy: 100%
- Correct out-of-fold predictions: 24/24
- Precision: 1.00 for both classes
- Recall: 1.00 for both classes
- F1-score: 1.00 for both classes

Confusion Matrix:

```
                 Predicted
                 Non-lesional  Lesional
Actual Non-lesional    12        0
Actual Lesional         0       12
```

These results demonstrate excellent classification performance within the available dataset under patient-grouped cross-validation.

### 2. Permutation Test

A preliminary permutation test was performed to assess whether the observed classification accuracy could be reproduced when class labels were randomly reassigned.

The test used 20 random permutations, preserving the paired patient structure by randomly swapping lesional and non-lesional labels within patients.

Results:
- Original accuracy: 100.00%
- Mean permuted accuracy: 44.17%
- Maximum permuted accuracy: 62.50%
- Empirical p-value: 0.0476

None of the 20 permuted datasets achieved the original model accuracy.

Interpretation:

The observed decrease in performance after label permutation supports the presence of a relationship between gene expression patterns and tissue classification.

However, the permutation test is preliminary. With only 20 permutations, 0.0476 is the minimum attainable empirical p-value. Additional permutations would provide a more precise statistical estimate.

### 3. Metadata Consistency Check

Sample metadata was inspected to verify patient pairing and class labels.

Findings:
- All 12 patients have exactly two samples.
- Each patient has one lesional and one non-lesional sample.
- Sample identifiers and tissue labels are consistently organized.
- Lesional samples consistently precede non-lesional samples in the dataset.

The available processed metadata contains four fields:
- sample_id
- sample_title
- patient_id
- tissue_type

No batch identifiers or processing dates are included in the current metadata table.

This check confirms the expected paired sample structure but does not rule out potential technical confounding.

### 4. Current Model Capabilities

The model classifies skin tissue samples as lesional or non-lesional based on microarray gene expression measurements.

It does not analyze clinical photographs or diagnose acne from images.

The model has demonstrated successful out-of-fold classification within GSE53795.

It has not yet been evaluated on an independent external dataset.

### 5. Limitations

- Small dataset: 12 patients and 24 samples.
- High-dimensional input: 54,675 probe sets.
- All samples originate from the same study.
- Potential batch effects and technical confounding have not been fully excluded.
- The preliminary permutation test includes only 20 permutations.
- External generalization remains unknown.
- Classification performance does not establish which biological mechanisms cause acne.
- The model is not clinically validated and is not intended for diagnostic or treatment decisions.

### 6. Next Step — External Validation

The next development stage is to evaluate the model on an independent gene expression dataset.

Planned procedure:

1. Train the complete preprocessing and classification pipeline on all 24 samples from GSE53795.
2. Obtain an independent dataset containing lesional and non-lesional acne skin samples.
3. Verify compatibility of microarray features and preprocessing methods.
4. Apply the trained pipeline to the external samples without fitting it on external labels or samples.
5. Compare predictions against the external ground-truth labels.
6. Calculate accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrix.
7. Assess whether classification performance generalizes beyond the original study.

The external dataset will remain independent during evaluation.
